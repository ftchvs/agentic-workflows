# Locale voice rules

These are starter rules. Replace them with rules that match your audience and
brand voice.

## Portuguese (Brazil) `pt-BR`

- Use natural Brazilian Portuguese.
- Prefer conversational `voce` over formal constructions.
- Keep common tech terms in English when Brazilian tech readers use them:
  `workflow`, `framework`, `dashboard`, `API`, `LLM`, `prompt`.
- Avoid stiff phrases such as `nao hesite em`.
- Use direct verbs and short sentences when the English source is punchy.

## Spanish `es`

- Use informal `tu` for a modern technical audience unless the site requires
  formal `usted`.
- Keep common tech and marketing loanwords when natural: `dashboard`,
  `workflow`, `API`, `framework`, `marketers`, `GTM`.
- Avoid literal English sentence order when Spanish needs a smoother sequence.
- Prefer clear, direct phrasing over ornate translation.

## French `fr`

- Choose one register and stay consistent. For product writing, informal `tu`
  can work; for institutional writing, use `vous`.
- Keep common tech terms in English when the community expects them:
  `workflow`, `framework`, `dashboard`, `API`.
- Avoid word-for-word translations of English metaphors.
- Preserve sharp claims without making them sound bureaucratic.

## Japanese `ja`

- Use natural Japanese sentence order and omit subjects when context makes them
  obvious.
- Use polite but not overly formal `です/ます` style for broad professional
  content.
- Keep product names in their original form.
- Use katakana for common loanwords when natural.
- It is acceptable for sentence count and order to differ from the English
  source if the meaning and structure are preserved.

## German `de`

- Use informal `du` for modern creator, startup, or technical content unless the
  brand requires formal `Sie`.
- Keep common tech nouns in English or Germanized English when natural:
  `Workflow`, `Framework`, `Dashboard`, `API`, `Prompt`.
- Capitalize nouns.
- Avoid heavy compound nouns when they make the prose feel less human.
